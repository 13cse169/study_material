01 Foundation

Introduction to AI & ML

Before diving into Generative AI, you need to understand the landscape it sits in. AI is the broad field; ML and DL are the methods.

Artificial Intelligence

AI is the science of building machines that can perform tasks typically requiring human intelligence — recognising speech, making decisions, translating languages.

💡 Analogy: AI is the umbrella. Everything else lives under it.

Machine Learning

ML is a subset of AI where systems learn from data rather than following hand-written rules. They find patterns and improve over time without explicit programming.

💡 A spam filter that learns which emails are junk is classic ML.

Deep Learning

DL is ML using neural networks with many layers. These layers extract increasingly complex features from raw data — enabling image recognition, translation, and more.

💡 DL is why your phone can identify faces in photos.

Neural Networks

Inspired by the brain, neural networks are stacked layers of mathematical nodes. Data enters the input layer, gets transformed through hidden layers, and produces output.

  • Input layer — receives raw data
  • Hidden layers — learn representations
  • Output layer — final prediction

02 Core Concept

Generative AI

Generative AI is a class of models that create new content — text, images, audio, code, video — rather than just classifying or predicting.

 What is Generative AI?

Generative AI models are trained on massive datasets and learn the underlying distribution of data. They can then sample from that distribution to generate brand-new, realistic outputs.

💡 Key idea: Rather than asking "Is this a cat?", GenAI asks "What does a cat look like?" — and draws one.

03 Under the Hood

How Generative AI Works

You don't need a PhD to understand the core ideas. Here are the four concepts that explain almost everything.

Transformers

The transformer is the architecture behind almost all modern LLMs. Its key innovation is the attention mechanism — it lets the model weigh how relevant each word is to every other word, regardless of distance.

💡 "The bank can guarantee deposits will eventually cover future tuition costs" — attention helps the model know "bank" = financial institution, not river bank.

Tokens & Embeddings

Text is split into tokens (roughly word-pieces). Each token is converted into a vector (embedding) — a list of numbers that encodes meaning. Similar concepts have similar vectors.

  • "Hello world" → [15496, 995] (token IDs)
  • King − Man + Woman ≈ Queen (embedding math)

Training vs Inference

  • Training: Model sees billions of examples, adjusts billions of parameters to minimise prediction error. Weeks on thousands of GPUs.
  • Inference: You send a prompt → model runs a forward pass → returns a response. Milliseconds to seconds.
💡 Training is like school; inference is using what you learned.

Probability-Based Generation

LLMs don't look things up — they predict the next most likely token given everything before it. The model assigns a probability to every possible next word and samples from that distribution.

💡 "The sky is ___" → P(blue)=0.45, P(clear)=0.20, P(red)=0.05 … The model picks blue, but not always.

04 Core Technology

Large Language Models

LLMs are the engines powering ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and most of modern Generative AI. Here's what you actually need to know.

What are LLMs?

Large Language Models are transformer-based neural networks trained on internet-scale text data (hundreds of billions of tokens). They learn language patterns, facts, reasoning, and code — all from next-token prediction.

  • GPT-4 — ~1 trillion parameters (estimated)
  • LLaMA 3 — 8B to 70B parameters (open)
  • Gemini Ultra — multimodal (text + image)

How LLMs Generate Text

  1. You send a prompt (your input)
  2. It's tokenised and embedded into vectors
  3. Passes through ~96 transformer layers
  4. Each layer refines understanding with attention
  5. Final layer outputs probability over vocabulary
  6. Sample next token → repeat until done

Context Window & Tokens

The context window is the maximum amount of text the model can "see" at once — your prompt + its response. GPT-3.5 = 16K tokens, GPT-4o = 128K tokens, Gemini 1.5 Pro = 1M tokens.

1 token ≈ 0.75 words. "Hello, world!" ≈ 4 tokens. A 128K context window can hold roughly a 300-page book.

Limitations

Hallucinations

Models confidently produce false information. They don't "know" facts — they predict plausible text.

Bias

Training data reflects societal biases. Models can reproduce stereotypes and unfair representations.

Knowledge Cutoff

LLMs have a training cutoff date. They don't know about events after that date without tools.

Cost

Running inference at scale is expensive. GPT-4 costs significantly more per token than GPT-3.5.

05 Essential Skill

Prompt Engineering

Prompt engineering is the art of communicating with LLMs effectively. The same question phrased differently can produce wildly different results.

 Zero-Shot Prompting

Ask the model directly without any examples. Works well for common tasks where the model already has strong priors.

INPUT
"Translate 'Good morning' to French."
OUTPUT
"Bonjour"

 Few-Shot Prompting

Provide 2–5 examples of input→output pairs before your actual question. The model learns the pattern from context.

INPUT
Positive: "I love this!" → 😊
Negative: "I hate it." → 😞
"This is okay." → ?
OUTPUT
😐

 Chain-of-Thought

Tell the model to think step by step. Dramatically improves performance on math, logic, and multi-step reasoning tasks.

INPUT
"If I have 5 apples and give away 2, then buy 3 more, how many do I have? Think step by step."
OUTPUT
Start: 5. After giving: 5−2=3. After buying: 3+3=6. Answer: 6

Best Practices

🎯
Be Specific

Vague prompts get vague answers. Include format, length, tone, and audience.

🎭
Assign a Role

"Act as a senior Python developer" frames the model's expertise and style.

📋
Give Context

More context = better output. Include background, constraints, and examples.

🔄
Iterate

Rarely perfect on the first try. Refine, rephrase, and experiment.

📏
Specify Format

"Respond in JSON", "Write a numbered list", "In under 50 words".

🧪
Test Variations

Small wording changes can produce significantly different outputs.

Python — Simple Chatbot with Memory 
from openai import OpenAI

client = OpenAI()
conversation_history = [
    {"role": "system", "content": "You are a friendly AI tutor."}
]

def chat(user_message):
    conversation_history.append({
        "role": "user",
        "content": user_message
    })
    response = client.chat.completions.create(
        model="gpt-4o-mini",
        messages=conversation_history
    )
    reply = response.choices[0].message.content
    conversation_history.append({
        "role": "assistant",
        "content": reply
    })
    return reply

# Interactive loop
while True:
    user_input = input("You: ")
    if user_input.lower() == "quit":
        break
    print(f"AI: {chat(user_input)}")

08 Critical Thinking

Limitations & Challenges

Every practitioner must understand the failure modes of Generative AI. These are not edge cases — they are everyday concerns.

Risk

Hallucinations

LLMs generate text that sounds correct but is factually wrong. They cannot distinguish between facts they learned and plausible-sounding fabrications.

⚠️ Example: An LLM asked for a paper citation may invent a convincing but non-existent paper title and author.
Risk

Bias & Fairness

Models trained on internet text inherit its biases — gender stereotypes, racial assumptions, political slants. These biases manifest in output and recommendations.

Cost

Cost & Resources

Training a frontier model costs $10M–$100M+. Inference at scale is expensive. Energy consumption is a growing environmental concern.

Technical

Data & Privacy

Models may memorise and regurgitate private training data. Using proprietary data for fine-tuning raises confidentiality and copyright concerns.

Interview Preparation

Generative AI is a branch of artificial intelligence focused on creating new content — text, images, audio, code, or video — rather than just classifying or analysing existing data.

It works by training large models on massive datasets to learn the patterns and distributions of data. Once trained, these models can generate brand-new outputs that resemble the training data.

Examples: ChatGPT generates text, DALL·E generates images, Suno generates music, GitHub Copilot generates code.

AI (Artificial Intelligence) is the broad goal of making machines that can simulate human intelligence. It's the umbrella term.

ML (Machine Learning) is a method to achieve AI — systems that learn from data rather than following explicit rules. A spam filter that improves over time is ML.

Deep Learning is a subset of ML using multi-layer neural networks. It's responsible for most recent breakthroughs — image recognition, language translation, generative AI.

Every DL system is ML, every ML system is AI — but not vice versa.

  • ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini — LLM-powered text generation and conversation
  • DALL·E 3 / Midjourney / Stable Diffusion — Image generation from text prompts
  • GitHub Copilot — Code generation and completion
  • Suno / ElevenLabs — Music and voice synthesis
  • Sora — Video generation from text

A token is the basic unit of text that LLMs process. Text is split into tokens before being fed into the model. A token is roughly 0.75 words in English.

"Hello, world!" ≈ 4 tokens. "Generative AI is transforming industries." ≈ 7 tokens.

Token count matters because: (1) it determines if your input fits in the context window, and (2) most APIs charge per token.

LLMs are transformer-based neural networks trained using self-supervised learning on massive text corpora. The training objective is simple: predict the next token given all previous tokens.

  1. Text is tokenised into numeric IDs
  2. Token IDs are converted to dense vectors (embeddings)
  3. Multiple attention layers process the sequence, learning relationships between all tokens
  4. The final layer outputs a probability distribution over the vocabulary
  5. The highest probability token is selected (or sampled), appended to output, and the process repeats

After pre-training, models are fine-tuned using RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) to align with human preferences — making them helpful, harmless, and honest.

Prompt engineering is the practice of designing inputs to LLMs to elicit the best possible outputs. Since LLMs are extremely sensitive to phrasing, how you ask a question is as important as what you ask.

Key techniques:

  • Zero-shot: Direct question, no examples
  • Few-shot: 2–5 input/output examples before the question
  • Chain-of-thought: "Think step by step" for complex reasoning
  • Role prompting: "You are an expert X…"
  • Format specification: "Respond in JSON / bullet points / under 100 words"

RAG is a technique that combines a retrieval system with an LLM to ground the model's responses in factual, up-to-date information from a knowledge base.

How it works:

  1. Convert your documents into embeddings and store in a vector database
  2. When a user asks a question, embed the query
  3. Find the most semantically similar document chunks
  4. Inject those chunks into the LLM prompt as context
  5. The LLM answers using both its trained knowledge and the retrieved context

RAG reduces hallucinations and enables LLMs to answer questions about private or recent data.

Fine-tuning is the process of further training a pre-trained model on a smaller, domain-specific dataset to specialise its behaviour.

When to fine-tune: When you need consistent formatting, style, tone, or domain-specific knowledge that can't be achieved through prompting alone.

Popular methods:

  • Full fine-tuning: Update all parameters (expensive)
  • LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation — train only a small number of adapter parameters (efficient)
  • RLHF: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback — used by OpenAI for ChatGPT

The Transformer (introduced in "Attention Is All You Need", 2017) replaced RNNs and LSTMs as the dominant sequence model. Its key innovation is self-attention.

Core components:

  • Multi-Head Self-Attention: Each token attends to all other tokens, computing a weighted sum based on relevance. Multiple attention heads capture different types of relationships.
  • Positional Encoding: Since attention has no inherent order, position information is added to embeddings.
  • Feed-Forward Layers: Applied independently to each position after attention.
  • Layer Normalisation & Residual Connections: Stabilise training of deep networks.

LLMs use only the decoder part of the original encoder-decoder transformer (GPT-style).

Embeddings are dense numerical representations (vectors) of words, sentences, or documents that encode semantic meaning. Similar concepts have vectors that are close together in high-dimensional space.

Why they matter:

  • Enable semantic search (find relevant content by meaning, not keywords)
  • Power RAG systems — embed documents and queries to find relevant chunks
  • Enable recommendation systems
  • Detect semantic similarity, clustering, classification

Example: text-embedding-3-small from OpenAI maps text to 1536-dimensional vectors. "dog" and "puppy" will be very close. "dog" and "quantum physics" will be far apart.

  • Hallucinations: Models confidently produce wrong information. Mitigation: RAG, grounding, output verification.
  • Latency: LLM inference is slow. Mitigation: streaming, smaller models, caching, batching.
  • Cost: Token costs add up at scale. Mitigation: prompt compression, smaller models for simpler tasks.
  • Safety & Guardrails: Preventing harmful outputs. Mitigation: system prompts, output filters, RLHF alignment.
  • Evaluation: Hard to measure LLM quality systematically. Mitigation: LLM-as-judge, human eval, evals frameworks.
  • Context management: Long conversations exceed context windows. Mitigation: summarisation, sliding window, vector memory.

Both control the randomness / creativity of LLM outputs by modifying how the model samples from its probability distribution.

Temperature: Scales the probability distribution. Low temperature (0.1–0.3) = deterministic, focused, repetitive. High temperature (0.8–1.2) = creative, varied, sometimes incoherent. Temperature=0 always picks the highest-probability token.

Top-p (nucleus sampling): Instead of scaling all probabilities, only sample from the smallest set of tokens whose cumulative probability exceeds p. Top-p=0.9 means sample from tokens that make up 90% of the probability mass, ignoring the long tail of unlikely tokens.

In practice: use temperature for overall creativity level, top-p for controlling the diversity of vocabulary. OpenAI recommends changing one, not both, at a time.
